# User Research Protocol

## Goal

Test whether people—including older adults—can understand a paused call and reach a safe verification path without being shamed, frightened, or required to understand AI.

This is usability research, not a real fraud simulation.

## Safety rules

- Obtain informed consent before every session.
- Use fictional names, fictional emergencies, and synthetic or consenting actor voices.
- Never clone a participant's relative or reuse private scam audio.
- Never request real money, credentials, OTPs, account details, or identity documents.
- Tell participants in advance that they may stop at any time.
- Debrief immediately and provide a clear explanation of the scenario.
- Store only consented observations; avoid raw audio/video unless essential.
- Do not recruit the recent victim from the motivating incident for a surprise or emotionally intense test.

## Three research conditions

1. **Ordinary call:** known or unknown caller with no consequential request.
2. **Uncertain request:** plausible caller requests information and accepts verification.
3. **Coercive request:** fictional emergency combines secrecy, urgency, and money.

## Tasks

Ask participants to:

1. Explain what they think the caller wants.
2. Decide what they would do next.
3. Use CallGate to verify through a known channel.
4. Explain why the system paused the request.
5. Find the option to change or remove a trusted contact.

## Questions

- In your own words, why did CallGate pause this call?
- What will happen if you press the main button?
- Did any message make you feel blamed or embarrassed?
- Did the system make you feel more or less in control?
- Who, if anyone, would you choose as a trusted verifier?
- Are there situations where notifying family would be unsafe?
- What information would you refuse to let CallGate store?

## Observe, do not lead

Record:

- Time to identify the requested action
- Time to start independent verification
- Misinterpretations of “verified” and “deepfake”
- Whether technical vocabulary creates hesitation
- Whether the person tries to proceed despite the pause
- Accessibility barriers: text size, hearing, language, motor actions
- Emotional response and signs that the session should stop

## Success criteria

Do not invent thresholds solely for a pitch. After an initial pilot, set and report explicit criteria such as:

- Participants can state the reason for the pause.
- Participants can identify that no payment/action has occurred.
- Participants can complete a known-channel verification flow.
- Participants do not interpret an acoustic flag as proof of guilt.
- Participants report feeling protected rather than judged.

Always publish sample size and limitations. A handful of friends is formative feedback, not population-level validation.
